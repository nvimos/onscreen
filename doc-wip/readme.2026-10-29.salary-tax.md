# Career session: skill read of the nvim config, salary in Norway, interview, tax

Highlights of the session of 2026-10-29.

## Skill read of the nvim config

- No IQ number can be read from a config. A forced guess (120–130) is just the typical range for experienced engineers;
  low confidence, ±15.
- Much of the code is likely AI-written, so code quality isn't evidence about the person.
- What counts: direction (what to build, requiring tests and a fallback load path), precise conventions in CLAUDE.md,
  judgment (upstream before patching, built-ins over dependencies), workflow design.
- Three weeks with a CLAUDE.md and a few skills is normal Claude Code use, not expertise.

## Salary target

- Oslo, senior developer or tech lead: about 850k–1.1M NOK. Trondheim: roughly 5–10% lower.
- 12 years (web dev → fullstack → backend → DevOps → operations; PHP, Go, some JS) in Trondheim: ask for about 900–950k,
  expect 850–950k.
- Figures are from training data (through about 2025); check kode24's lønnsundersøkelse for Trondheim and 10+ years.
- Up: Go, DevOps/platform, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, Kubernetes/cloud; product, energy, fintech.
- Down: a PHP-heavy title; QA or test automation titles (often 50–100k less).

## Interview

- Apply as senior backend, DevOps or platform engineer; test automation is the angle: "I build the test infrastructure
  that lets teams ship safely."
- Talk about decisions and trade-offs, not tools.
- Have two or three short stories: a bug traced into upstream code, a convention that improved things, a time you said no.
- AI use: sell learning speed, not expertise. Lead with the 12 years.
- Ask questions that show level: "How do you decide what gets tests?", "Who owns the developer tooling?"

## Negotiation in Norway

- Understatement over self-promotion; concrete stories carry the weight.
- Based on the salary surveys for Trondheim and 12 years in backend and DevOps, I'm looking at around 925k. Aim 5–10%
  above target.
- Negotiate the package: pension above the 2% minimum, overtime, home office, education budget.
- Base pay is hard to move later (raises often 3–5%); settle it before signing.

## Net pay

- Normal months are lower than the yearly average: tax is spread over 10.5 months; June (feriepenger) and December (half
  tax) pay more.
- Loan interest lowers tax by about 22% of the interest.
- Exact 2026 figures: skattekalkulator on skatteetaten.no.

## Pension (OTP) to check in the offer

From training data (G = grunnbeløpet, about 130k NOK in 2025); check current figures.

- Contribution rate: the legal minimum is 2%; good employers pay 5–7%. At 925k, 7% vs 2% is roughly 45–50k NOK a year.
- From which salary: from 0 kr or only above 1G. "From 0G" is better.
- Above 7.1G (about 925k): some schemes add up to 18.1% extra on salary between 7.1G and 12G. Worth asking about at this
  salary level.
- Ceiling: contributions usually stop at 12G (about 1.56M); not relevant at this level.
- Own share: check whether part of the contribution is taken from your pay.
- Investment and fees: which provider, whether you can choose the risk profile, and the yearly management fee.
- AFP: private sector employers with a collective agreement (tariffavtale) offer AFP, a lifelong top-up from 62. Ask
  whether they have it.
- Insurance in the package: disability pension (uførepensjon), survivor cover, health insurance.
- Count pension as part of the salary: a lower base with 7% from 0G can beat a higher base with 2%.
- Personal saving on top: IPS, up to 15,000 NOK a year, deductible at 22%.
