# Why consultant rates are 2–3× the salary-equivalent hourly rate

A consultant's hourly rate has to cover everything an employer normally pays on top of salary, plus the hours the consultant can't bill.

## Salary vs consultant cost (800,000 NOK/year, ~80 h of work)

| Basis | Rate per hour | 80 h |
|---|---|---|
| 800,000 ÷ 1,950 h (paid hours in a year) | ~410 NOK | ~33,000 |
| 800,000 ÷ ~1,700 h (hours worked after holidays) | ~470 NOK | ~38,000 |
| Employer cost, about 1.35× | ~550–640 NOK | ~45,000–51,000 |
| Consultant rate, 1,000–1,400 NOK/h | — | 80,000–110,000 |

## What the rate covers

1. **Paid hours vs billed hours**
   - An employee is paid for about 1,950 hours a year.
   - A consultancy expects to bill about 70–80% of those (about 1,400–1,500 hours). The rest goes to sales, bids, gaps between contracts, training and internal work.
   - Each billed hour also has to pay for the unbilled ones: about 1.3×.
2. **Employer costs**
   - Employer tax (14.1%), holiday pay (12%), pension (2–7%), insurance and sick pay: about 1.3–1.4× on top of salary.
   - When you buy a consultant, those costs move into the rate.
3. **The firm's own costs**
   - Office, equipment, licences, management, sales staff, HR, accounting, recruitment and certifications.
   - Typically 15–25%.
4. **Profit and risk**
   - A 10–20% margin.
   - The firm carries the risk: a consultant can be dropped at a week's notice, while the firm still pays its own employees.

## Rough multiplication

- Salary per paid hour: about 410 NOK.
- × 1.35 employer costs → about 550 NOK.
- ÷ 0.75 billable share → about 735 NOK.
- × 1.2 overhead → about 880 NOK.
- × 1.15 margin → about 1,000 NOK.

## Why buyers pay it

- **Flexibility:** no hiring process, no notice period, no long-term commitment. You pay for capacity only while you need it.
- **Speed:** someone starts next week rather than in 3–6 months.
- **Skills on demand:** a specialist for a short job, where hiring one full-time wouldn't pay off.
- **No downtime cost:** sick days, holidays, training and slow periods aren't on your bill.
- **Accounting:** an operating expense rather than more permanent staff.

Over a long period a consultant costs more than an employee. Firms that keep a consultant in the same role for years are usually paying for convenience or a headcount freeze rather than for any of the above.
